## WEEK 1: FEB 3-7

### Post 1 - Mon Feb 3: Discovery Prep
I've closed deals in 47 states.
And the best reps I know all do one thing consistently:
They treat the first discovery call like a research project, not a pitch session.

Before the call, I spend 5 minutes answering three questions:
1. What problem are they likely trying to solve?
2. Who else have they probably talked to?
3. What would make them walk away thinking "that was worth my time"?

That's it. 5 minutes.
Most reps spend 30 minutes prepping a demo.
I spend 5 minutes prepping to listen.
The deals close themselves after that.

**CTA:** What's your pre-call process look like?

### Post 2 - Tue Feb 4: The $65K Mistake
I lost a $65K deal last year because I didn't ask one question.
The deal was perfect on paper: Budget approved, Timeline set, Champion engaged, Stakeholders aligned.
Then radio silence. Three weeks later — went with a competitor.
Why? Their IT team had a bad experience with our platform 2 years ago. Before my time.
I never asked: "Have you worked with us before?"
One question. $65K mistake.
Now it's question #3 in every discovery call.

### Post 3 - Wed Feb 5: Always Be Discovering
Most sales advice is backwards.
"Always Be Closing." Nah. Always Be Discovering.
The best closers spend 70% of the sales cycle asking questions and 30% presenting solutions.
Bottom performers flip that ratio.
Discovery doesn't end after the first call.

**CTA:** How much of your sales cycle is spent discovering vs. presenting?

Speed doesn't mean desperate. Speed means you respect their time.

## WEEK 2: FEB 10-14

### Post 6 - Mon Feb 10: Why 3-5 Deals
Working 20 deals at once is just organized procrastination. I work 3-5.
I'm quick to disqualify. If they're not responding, not doing the bare minimum, they're probably not a good client.
20 deals x 10% attention = 2 deals of effort
5 deals x 100% attention = 5 deals of effort
Focus beats frenzy. Every time.

**CTA:** How many deals are you working right now?

### Post 7 - Tue Feb 11: Commission Breath
Buyers can smell when you need the deal. I call it "commission breath."
First call. Every time. I say:
"Hey, I've got a couple kids. Do I work on commission? Yes. Do I want to sell you as much stuff as possible? That'd be great. But that stuff's not important to you."
Why? Because it's honest. It removes the adversarial energy.

**CTA:** Have you ever addressed commission directly with a buyer?

### Post 8 - Wed Feb 12: Silence as Strategy
Bottom reps talk 72%. Top reps talk 46%. Data from 1M+ interactions.
Top reps ask better questions (not more). Listen to understand (not respond). Let silence do the work.
Ask the question. Then shut up. The awkward pause is where deals are won.

**CTA:** Do you struggle with silence on calls?

### Post 9 - Thu Feb 13: The Disqualification Mindset
The fastest way to hit quota? Stop working bad deals.
Every hour on a bad deal = an hour not spent on a good one.
Last year: 3 deals I should've disqualified earlier. 23 hours wasted.
Red flags: Not responding, can't get to decision maker, no compelling event, budget not confirmed, pain isn't quantified.

**CTA:** What's a deal you should've walked away from?

### Post 10 - Fri Feb 14: The Giving Philosophy
The moment you make the sale about you, you've lost.
Focus on: Give to the client. Help the client. Make their life easier.
Your quota? Not their problem. Your commission? Not their concern.
When you give without expectation, clients trust you. When they trust you, they buy.

**CTA:** What's one way you give value before asking for the sale?

### Post 12 - Wed Feb 19: The Three Questions Opener
"This call is not about me. This call is about you. My agenda is not important.
If we walked away today and I didn't answer the three questions you came with, it would be a total waste of your time. What are those three questions?"
This flips the script, surfaces what matters, and builds trust immediately.
